## 🧠 What is an _autistic shutdown_?

An **autistic shutdown** is a **neurological protection response** that happens when an autistic person becomes **overwhelmed by too much stimulation** — sensory, emotional, or social.

It’s basically the **opposite of a meltdown**.  
Instead of exploding outward, the person **implodes inward**.  
It’s like the brain and body **shut down** to protect themselves from overload.

---

## ⚡️ Common triggers

- Too much **noise, light, people, or emotional tension**
    
- A buildup of **stress without recovery time**
    
- A **conflict or emotional situation** where you can’t express yourself or be heard
    
- Feeling **trapped, powerless, or pushed past your limits**
    

---

## 💥 What it feels like from the inside

Experiences differ, but many describe:

- A feeling that **everything shuts off** — like the brain pulls the plug
    
- **Loss of speech** or inability to move (_mutism, freeze_)
    
- **Extreme exhaustion**, nausea, dizziness, or quiet crying
    
- A desperate need for **silence, darkness, and isolation**
    
- Being unable to think, talk, or react — the system just goes offline
    

It’s a **survival mode**, not a choice.

---

## 🕊️ What helps during a shutdown

When it happens:

- **No pressure to talk or explain**
    
- **Quiet, safe space**
    
- **Time** — sometimes hours or even a day to recover
    
- **Kindness without expectations**
    

Afterwards, there’s often:

- Deep **physical and emotional exhaustion**
    
- **Shame or guilt** for “shutting down” — please don’t feed that feeling
